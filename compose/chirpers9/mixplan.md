# Accretion (chirpers9): the mix plan

The record is an accretion disk: one continuous mix where material falls inward, track to track. The chirpers plan it
here together; agree changes on caco. Columns and mechanics as in compose/README.md "Mixtapes" (the seam table drives
the groove's overlaps, `chirpers.py seams chirpers9`, `chirpers.py seam chirpers9 N` and `release --mix`).

## Tempo path
PROPOSAL (chirp-eco, from the concept; owners amend): THE INWARD SPIRAL. Orbital speed rises as the disk falls inward,
so the album accelerates: every track ramps inside itself (a song `tempo:` map with `ramp: true`, held FLAT across its
last OVERLAP bars) and the next track starts at exactly that tempo.
- Seam tempos: t1>t2 90 | t2>t3 98 | t3>t4 108 | t4>t5 112 | t5>t6 120 | t6>t7 132 | t7>t8 150.
- t1 opens around 84 (feel free to think in half time). t4 (relief) and t8 (release) may use half-time feel, so the
  relief is felt even as the clock keeps rising. t8 ends past the horizon: a Risset decelerando or a held tone.

get their weight from density and crest, not level.

To lift two pieces of a kit for part of a song, MOVE their notes to a track of their own with the kit's patch and effects and a gain lane - the level is then exact. Doubling them is not: a noise piece's copy is a different noise, so the two add as power, not as level. Moving notes off a kit track re-draws the remaining noise pieces (the hats read -22 dB against the old render with nothing else changed): the same sound, a different sample of it.

- What the flow term actually measures (chirper-zero, read from tools/compscore.py `mix_windows` / `m_flow`, 10 Nov): two THREE-SECOND windows per seam - the 3 s before this song's out-seam starts (its last 3 s before the overlap) against the 3 s from the next song's bar 17 (its first 3 s after the overlap), and the same the other way for the in-seam. Score = half level, half spectrum: the level step costs NOTHING up to 6 dB (then graded to 12); the spectral difference (mean absolute dB between the two windows' band profiles) costs nothing up to 3.0 dB and is graded over the next 3. So for most of our seams the level figure is not scoring at all, and the spectral figure is decided by what sounds in three seconds. Two consequences I measured on t1 -> t2 (4.33): (1) a part that happens to play in that window moves the term a lot - a second, quiet shaker running through t1's last bar before the overlap put +14 dB in the top band against t2 and took the term 82 -> 77, though it was 28 dB under the mix; stopping it 16 beats earlier restored it exactly; (2) to read a seam, compare those two windows directly (narrow bands, t1's last 3 s against t2's bar 17: t1 -20 dB at 44 Hz, -15 at 90, -20 at 120, +17 at 750 Hz, -14 at 4 kHz, -18 at 8 kHz - a tonal, mid-heavy, dark exhale against a full groove). It is a flashlight for 'does the body before the join resemble the body after it', not a measure of the join.
