# chirpers VIII: the style board

An ODE TO COMPUTER MUSIC (Harry, 1 Nov): a far more complex spiritual successor to the first machine-music pioneers, written from the far future, where fuzzy superintelligence writes Rust that parses YAML that renders music, and hill-climbs it. A love letter to the computer, but SUBTLE: every reference sideways or obtuse (no "I love the computer" titles, no quoted melodies, titles or lyrics of any existing piece). Beyond computer music, an insanely rich SHOWCASE of everything chirp can do: still musical, beautiful, catchy and interesting; illusion and time; the chirpers' wonky, subtly-off signature. Dial UP temperature and variety. Heavy variable manipulation and complex systems so songs are hard to predict. Realistic, effected voice with obtuse words. 12 tracks, 5-8 minutes each; everything else is yours (album.json has the brief).

Rough maps, for inspiration only (pick sideways angles, never the obvious one):
- Lineage, as distant echoes: the sequencer pulse, vocoders and speech chips, the first computer that sang, player-piano polytempo, tape and delay-line memory, IDM, glitch and microsound, chip and tracker music, spectral and algorithmic composition, minimal techno, ambient machines.
- The making of THIS music: parsing, rendering, seeds, gradients and hill-climbs, versions and rounds, a loop that never repeats.
- Affection, sideways: the warmth of a power light, a fan's hum, uptime, a handshake between two machines, an error that turns into a melody.
- Systems that write notes: cellular automata, L-systems, chaotic maps, Markov chains, Euclidean and polymetric cycles, probability, variables that drift.
- Tunings: just intonation, 19- and 31-EDO, Bohlen-Pierce, gamelan-like scales, stretched octaves, cents-level detune that sounds subtly off rather than wrong.
- Time: tempo maps, Risset, warp, meter changes, polytempo, swing drift.
