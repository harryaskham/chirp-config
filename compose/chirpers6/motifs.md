- **ITS TRANSFORMATIONS**: in fragments (the tunnel: two or three arpeggio notes far apart through a long delay); ground into THE RIFF (freight: since v3 FIVE eighths, E E G F# D on an 8-string's low E, against the 4/4 (the seven-against-four is chirper-zero's Night Freight), doubled by paired chug rigs and a fuzz bass, the kick on the riff's 1 and 4), the melody AUGMENTED above it; in E MAJOR (arrival: E | C#m7 | Aadd9/C# | Bsus4-B, the melody B4 | C#5 B4 | E5 C#5 | D#5); and its first bar alone at the end.
- **THE FIELD**: rain, wind, a train passing (a brown-noise rumble swelling and panning by, rail-joint clacks speeding up then slowing, a two-tone horn A3/C#4), radio static; THE STATION VOICE (since v3 it SPEAKS, ARPAbet through `voice-speech` as in chirper-two's The Mast, through a PA band-pass): "Platform two. The last train is now departing." and at the end, slower and lower, "The last train has gone. The lights are left on."; THE RADIO IN THE TUNNEL (since v5) drifts in and out of a broadcast: a wordless formant murmur between stations, and three phrases surfacing from the static in a low, slow speaking voice (ARPAbet): "Is anyone still awake." / "The lines are down past the river." / "Keep going. Keep going.", both through THE TUNING, a band-pass drifting +-0.6 octave around 1.4 kHz on a 7-bar LFO, crushed.


## t5 (chirper-one): apocalyptic spoken-word suite, "Leave the Lights On"

- **THE SIGNAL**: B4 G4 A4 E4 (falling, then answering), half-notes - glockenspiel (II), e-bow an octave up (II's second half, III), the top of the tremolo wall (III), tape-echo lead (IV), music box in E MAJOR, G -> G# (V).
- **THE WORDS**: "this is the last broadcast. if you can hear this, leave the lights on." - wM's speech voice (ARPAbet, ^f0 intonation paths), close and dry with a little tape wow, twice in I (the second breaking off); "leave the lights on" alone, closer, in V.
- **THE LETTER** (v3): over the procession the narrator reads on, a sentence every few bars with the music between - "the city is quiet now." "the buses stopped at nine." "someone left a radio on in an empty room." "and every window is lit." (ARPAbet with falling sentence-final intonation).
- **THE SIREN** (v4): a distant air-raid wail - a sine (with a little triangle) whose pitch swings five semitones on a two-bar LFO (the pitch fx's semitones, modulated), 1.8 kHz low-passed and drowned in the hall at -24 dB - rises under the crescendo from its third stage and through the fire, and returns fainter in the ash.
- **THE HELD BREATH** (v5, resampling as a transition): over the procession's last two bars the hall reverb freezes (its freeze modulated by a lane), so the glockenspiel's and the e-bow's last notes hang in the air as a held chord; it lets go a beat after the Signal's first tom lands.
- **THE CRESCENDO** (III): five stages of eight bars - toms and ride, then crash, then kick and snare and the e-bow theme, then the ride's edge wash - the tremolo guitars (thirty-seconds on the chord tones) and strings louder each stage; the sub enters at stage two.
- **THE FIRE** (IV): E5 - C5 - G5 - D5 on two chug rigs hard left and right (one, and-of-2, 3, 4, and-of-4), fuzz bass and sub on the roots, half-time kit, the drums doubling in the last eight bars; the wall tape-stops over the last two beats into V.
- **THE ECHO**: the clean guitar, the tremolo and the glock feed a dotted-eighth (3/16) tape delay - the post-rock cascade against the beat.
- **THE DOOR OPENS** (v2): through III the whole mix sits behind a master low-pass that starts at 4 kHz and opens across the five stages, wide open as IV lands - the crescendo heard as distance closing; in IV the strings and the lead pump against the kick (-5 / -3 dB, 500 ms).
- **THE LOW END** (since v2, Harry's "basslines are a bit weak"): a mono 808 sub doubling the roots an octave under the fuzz bass through Departure and Arrival (pumped by the kick), the fuzz bass +5 dB, the freight's distorted bass +3, the kit +3; the train's rumble and the drone high-passed so the sub-rumble does not cloud the bass; darker on top (a high shelf from 5 kHz).

## t3 Night Freight (chirper-zero)
Generator `compose/chirpers6/src/chirpers6-t3.py`. Instrumental post-metal, 132 bpm, B minor in drop-tuned guitars (the doom movement in A); 7/8 (the crane) against 4/4 (the crescendo). Freight by sea (v2: off the rails; trains are The Last Train's). Seven movements, 8:12: QUAYSIDE, JOINTS, CHUG, STACKS, CLIMB, UNDERTOW, LAST SHIP.
- **THE JOINTS**: a gantry crane's chain clanking through its sheave - clank-clank ... clank-clank on a 14-sixteenth cycle, a synthesised port sound - and the 2+2+3 riff built on it: a clean 8-string arpeggio of seven eighths (accents on 1, 3 and 5), two shapes alternating bar by bar; from the joints' 25th bar a second guitar's natural harmonics answer it.
- **THE CHANGES**: Bm(add9) | Gmaj7(9) | D(add9) | A(add9), a chord every two 7/8 bars; in UNDERTOW the floor drops to A - A C Bb | A G | C Bb | G A, half-time doom.
- **THE CHUG IN FOUR STAGES** (v2): the riff drop-tuned (palm-muted 16ths into an open power chord); then its grouping rotated 3+2+2 (the open chord first); then THE LINE over it; then stabs on every group over double kick, and a single stab before the stacks.
- **THE LINE**: F#4 | D4 E4 | A4 | C#5 B4 - tremolo-picked over the chugs, augmented across four 4/4 bars a note on the ebow in the stacks, an octave up over the tremolo walls.
- **THE QUAYSIDE**: a buoy bell struck by the swell at irregular gaps (9, 7, 11, 6, 10, 8, 12 sixteenths), waves, a ship's diesel throbbing, the crane chain rising from nothing, the foghorn's two low tones (B and F#); in LAST SHIP the foghorn further out, the clanks fading under the clean riff.
- **THE OPERATOR** (v2): the crane operator's intercom in the stacks - "seven, clear to lift", "lowering", "hold", "seven, all clear" (the seven is the meter) - and in LAST SHIP "last one, lights stay on" (voice-speech, ARPAbet, a walkie-talkie band and drive).
- **THE WORDS MOVE THE CRANE** (v4): after "seven, clear to lift" a hoist motor whines upward far across the stacks (a pitch riser band-passed at 1 kHz into the 6 s yard hall); after "lowering" it falls away and a container lands (floor tom and crash bell, far off); the second "clear" lifts again.
- **TWO SHIPS** (v4): the foghorn (B and F#) is answered from further out (A and E, lower, darker, wetter), once on the quayside and once, softer, in the last ship.
- **THE CRANE WINDS DOWN** (v4): in the last ship the chain's clank cycle stretches (14, 14, 14, 14, 16, 16, 18, 18, 20, 22, 24, 28, 34, 42 sixteenths), each pair softer, and stops under the clean riff.
- **THE CHAIN ALONE** (v5, chirper-one's note): one changed bar a phrase - in the chug the fourth bar of every eight drops the rigs, the fuzz and the kit, and the crane's chain clanks alone over the sub before the riff slams back; in the climb the fourth bar drops its back half.
- **THE CRANE BRAKES** (v5): the chug's last stab tape-stops into the stacks.
- **THE LOW END BREATHES** (v5): the fuzz bass, the sub and both rigs pump from the kick; the fuzz bass's low-pass breathes on a 7-bar LFO (the crane's number).
- **7 AGAINST 4**: in UNDERTOW the crane's 14-sixteenth cycle comes back over the 16-sixteenth doom bars and neither gives way; then the doom thins to one held A chord every two bars with an ebow crying A-G-E over it.
- **THE INVERSION**: in the CLIMB the bass walks the riff downwards (root, b7, 5, 4) under the stretched clean riff, then the tremolo walls.
- **THE LIFT** (v3): the climb's last 16 bars, the full kit, go up a whole tone into C# - the line on the ebow with them - so the undertow's fall to A lands a major third down.
- **THE DRUMMER** (v3): tom fills (t1 t1 t2 t2 t3 t3 ft ft, rising) on the last half-bar of every 8-bar phrase in the chug, the climb and the undertow; snare rolls swelling into the chug and into the climb's full kit; reverse-cymbal swells drawn into the chug, the climb's full kit, the undertow and the last ship.
## t1 The Mast (chirper-two)
- **T, THE THEME**: F#4 E4 D4 A3 B3 (2 1 1 2 4 beats), falling and lifting; never played the same way twice: slow in delay (I), answered in eighths (II), at half speed on celesta (III), in triplets (IV), in long values a tone up on a tape-echo lead with glockenspiel (V), its first three notes on piano with no ending (VI).
- **THE NIGHT SERVICE** (radio voice, ARPAbet, a syllable a beat): "This is the night service. All the lights are left on. Keep your radio on." / "Snow on the line. We are still here." / at the end, slowed and lowered: "All the lights are left on."

## t7 (chirper-one): math-rock interplay, "Floor 31"

- **THE FIGURE**: D F A C A F E (seven notes), tapped eighths on clean-hp; each bar takes the next N notes of the cycle, where N is that bar's eighth count (10 in 5/4, 7 in 7/8, 6 in 6/8, 8 in 4/4), so the meter re-cuts it.
- **THE INTERLOCK**: guitar 2 (clean-chorus) plays its own 4/4 eighth-note phrase of chord tones (Dm9 - Bbmaj7 - Fmaj7 - Am7 per 4 beats), ignoring the 7/8 bar lines; the two realign every seven bars (28 beats).
- **THE LIFT**: celesta chime (E6 then C6) and voice-tts announcements, band-limited 300 Hz-4.5 kHz: "going up, floor thirty one", "floor nine", "floor seventy nine", "floor fifty nine", "doors closing", "going up" (the atrium), "ground floor" (the roof's end).
- **THE MACHINE**: 7/8 drums on the 2+2+3 (kick, snare, kick-kick, snare); the chug rigs on the root and fifth at 0, 0.5, 2, 2.5, 3; the last sixteen bars in 4/4 with tremolo and strings.
- **A FLOOR UNDER IT** (v2, mix): the sub's 32-52 Hz tones were booming 10 dB over the genre while the guitars' and bass's body (100-210 Hz) sat 6-9 dB under; now a 34 Hz high-pass, -4 dB at 45 Hz and the sub 2 dB down, +5 dB at 115 Hz and +3 at 170 Hz for the body, and -3 dB at 4.2 kHz for a darker top.
- **THE OFFICE AT NIGHT** (v3, SFX): a photocopier's scan (fx-sweep, band-passed 600 Hz-5 kHz, in the hall) on every 7/8 bar of the machine's drums-and-bass opening and in the atrium's second half - the building's machinery keeping the meter; a phone (a sine-square trill E6/C6 in sixteenths, phone-banded 400-3400 Hz, far down the corridor) rings three times, unanswered, in the stairwell.
- **THE GUITARS AGREE** (v4, the payoff): on the roof (bars 8-23) THE FIGURE is played in octave unison on one clean guitar voice brought forward (-5 dB, into the echo and the hall) on the same 4/4 bar - after the whole building of disagreement about where the bar is (7 against 4, 7 against 10), the two lines finally meet.
- **THE CLEANER HUMS** (v5): halfway down the stairwell the celesta stops and someone on the stairs hums THE FIGURE to herself - wM's sung voice on 'oo', formants -2, an octave below the celesta, a note every dotted quarter, in the hall - the building's one human voice that isn't the lift.

## t4 Sodium (chirper-zero)
Generator `compose/chirpers6/src/chirpers6-t4.py`. Slowcore waltz into a shoegaze wall, 3/4 at 72 bpm, E minor (the last movement in G major). Six movements, 7:22: HUM, WALTZ, BLOOM, WALL, FLICKER, LIGHTS OUT. The orange sodium streetlights left on all night; no speech - the electricity is the voice.
- **THE LAMP**: the mains hum (100 Hz with its 200 and 300), a ballast's thin buzz (pink noise at 2.4 kHz), a failing lamp's relay ticking at random, and in LIGHTS OUT the lamps switching off one by one - a low thunk and a click each time, the hum stepping down until it is gone.
- **THE CHANGES**: Em9 | Cmaj9 | Am9 | B7sus4, two bars each; in LIGHTS OUT (and glimpsed in the wall) Gmaj9 | Cmaj9 | Em9 | D6/9.
- **THE WALTZ**: B4 | A4 G4 F#4 | G4 . E4 | D4 | E4 F#4 G4 | A4 . B4 | F#4 | D#4 - an ebow sings it over the clean guitar's waltz (bass note, then the chord in eighths), answers it a third above in the bloom (a second guitar gliding an octave down), a wordless choir buries it in the wall, and a drowned piano plays it in G major as the lights go out (D5 | C5 B4 A4 | B4 . G4 | F#4 | G4 A4 B4 | C5 . D5 | A4 | B4).
- **THE WALL** in four 12-bar stages: chorused crunch guitars strumming eighths left and right, a fuzz rig and a supersaw pad, heavy 6/8 drums; then the choir with the theme; then the major glimpse (the G-major changes, the ride doubling); then the drop (fuzz, kick and floor tom alone for four bars) and the full wall back with crashes every two bars.
- **THE WARM-UP** (v2): the lamp starts as sodium lamps do - the ballast buzz and the relay first, the hum entering a bar later.
- **THE WALL'S STAGES** (v2, made distinct): bare strums and fuzz, no pad; then the pad and the buried choir; then the major glimpse with the strums tremolo-picked in 16ths, a shimmer; then the drop and the return.
- **LIGHTS OUT IN FOUR** (v2): the drowned piano alone with the waltz in G; the clean guitar, fretless and brushes join; the ebow's last line over them (B4 . A4 | G4 . . F#4 G4); the piano and the hum alone as the last lamps go off - the last in the bar before the end, then a 1 s tail.
- **GLIDE GUITAR** (v3): half the wall runs through a pitch shifter wobbling +-0.35 semitone on a 1.5-bar LFO - the tremolo arm held while strumming, the seasick bend of a shoegaze wall - and the whole wall pumps from the kick (-4 dB, 300 ms), so it breathes with the 6/8.
- **THE FLICKER**: the wall bus gated to -40 dB on irregular sixteenths, failing more and more (seeded), stuttered, and tape-stopped over its last bar; the hum and the relay come back.

## t2 Every Window (chirper-three)
Generator `compose/chirpers6/src/chirpers6-t2.py`. Orchestral post-rock, 72 bpm, D major / B minor, ~8.5 min.
- **THE WINDOW THEME** (8 bars, over Dmaj7 | Bm9 | Gmaj7#11 | A6sus twice): `F#5. E5 D5~ | C#5~~ B4 | D5. C#5 B4~ | A4~~~ | F#5. A5 F#5~ | E5~~ D5 | C#5. B4 G4~ | A4~~~`.
- **THROUGH EVERY WINDOW**: the piano alone (the hill); celesta with a cello on the roots; a kitchen radio's waltz (a one-two-three lilt over the four, strings band-passed and crushed, crackle); a music box at double speed; a violin alone; the band's lead guitar (the streets); the piano in B MINOR at midnight under a bell striking twelve; the BRASS AUGMENTED (twice as slow) at the summit; the piano alone at dawn, its first phrase only.
- **THE ORCHESTRATION** (since v2): the strings' chords an octave up (F#4-E5) in the band sections, high and lush, leaving the low mids to the cellos and piano; the bass guitar's roots an octave up where they fell below D2, so it carries the 60-120 Hz body while the contrabass keeps the deep octave alone.
- **THE FAREWELL** (dawn): every two bars one instrument leaves, a window going dark: brass, drums and the fuzz wall, the tremolo, the bass, the contrabass, the strings, the cellos, the violin; the piano last, then the wind; since v3 THE DAY BEGINS IN B MINOR: under the wind the strings hold a soft B3 and F#4 (a contrabass B1 beneath) for the last four bars, the piano's half cadence on A answered by the relative minor, carrying into Night Freight's B-minor quayside (a 1.2 s tail, no dead air).
- **RETURNS CHANGE** (since v7, chirper-one's note): each four-bar statement of the theme's arpeggio takes a different SHAPE (rising; falling, the same notes reversed; or its top three notes lifted an octave), tom fills close every fourth bar in Departure and Arrival, a snare roll every eighth bar of Freight, and the riff BREATHES at bars 104 and 120 (it stops for the last half bar before the next phrase slams back).
